About Robyn

A student, teacher, and referee who keeps finding new reasons to learn.

Her story

Finding a lifelong practice

Robyn’s connection to martial arts reaches back to the way she saw the world at ten years old. She still tries to tap into that younger self when she thinks about what first drew her to karate: the girl who liked the color black and the movie Sidekicks. Today, Robyn practices Okinawan Goju-Ryu karate and taekwondo. Both include sparring, kicks, punches, and blocks, though their languages and classroom approaches differ. The schools where she trains emphasize sport martial arts and competition.

Robyn keeps returning for the different rewards training brings. She enjoys learning, the people who have become family, and the chance to inspire others. As a student, teacher, and referee, she tries to lead by example and help create a training environment where people can keep learning. She also values consistency and fairness in tournament circuits. Her first karate instructor helped her take pride in her skills and develop her drive. Women in martial arts have inspired her, too, by showing her that being unique can be a strength.

Her trip to Okinawa was a once-in-a-lifetime experience. It gave her a chance to connect with a deeper sense of heritage and glimpse something larger than herself. She especially remembers the masters who corrected her technique. Their close attention helped her see how she could keep improving, lead by example, and support students as they advance in rank.

Martial arts have given Robyn an extended family inside and outside the dojo. She began taekwondo in 1995 and karate in 2021, and says her training partners have supported her through both. Regular practice and greater awareness of her diet have also helped her live a healthier life. Now she is learning sword and working toward her black belt in karate.
